This section introduces the research interests of Dr. Gregor von Laszewski, the instructor of this course. His work focuses on the intersection of cloud infrastructure, distributed systems, and the quantitative analysis of scientific impact.

## Primary Research Themes

Dr. von Laszewski's research is centered on creating scalable, efficient, and transparent cloud environments. Key areas of focus include:

*   **Cloud Computing Architectures**: Developing new models for resource management and scheduling in multi-cloud environments.
*   **Cloud and Big Data Integration**: Researching how to optimize the movement and processing of petabyte-scale datasets across distributed cloud nodes.
*   **Edge and Fog Computing**: Exploring the decentralization of compute power to reduce latency and bandwidth usage for IoT and real-time applications.
*   **Scientific Impact Analysis**: Developing metrics and frameworks (such as those integrated into XDMoD) to quantify the return on investment for high-end cyberinfrastructure based on publication and citation data.

## Collaboration and Independent Study

Dr. von Laszewski frequently collaborates with students through **independent studies**. These collaborations are governed by a commitment to openness: **all work produced is released as open-source projects**. This ensures that the research is reproducible, transparent, and provides a tangible contribution to the community.

If you are interested in pursuing an independent study or contributing to an ongoing research project, please reach out during office hours or via email to discuss potential topics.

## Academic Guidance

For students balancing their professional and academic lives, the following credit hour recommendations are suggested to ensure a sustainable learning pace:

*   **Online Students (with full-time employment)**: It is recommended not to exceed **6 credit hours** per semester.
*   **Residential Graduate Students (full-time)**: It is recommended not to exceed **9 credit hours** per semester.
