    Course Description¶

    Cloud Computing, DevOps, and AI is a hands-on, project-based course that introduces students to modern cloud infrastructure, DevOps practices, and the integration of AI into cloud-native applications. Students gain practical experience with technologies such as AWS, Kubernetes, Python scripting, containerization, and automation tools including Ansible and Jenkins, to name only a few. Throughout the semester, each student designs and implements an original project tailored to their interests, applying course concepts to solve a real-world problem. Projects may focus on areas such as cloud security, infrastructure automation, AI-powered operations, or scalable cloud services. Students define and shape the project within the scope of the class and instructor approval. The course emphasizes learning by doing, encourages creativity and independent exploration, and provides the flexibility for students to use public cloud free tiers or local environments while developing skills directly applicable to industry.

    Learning Outcome¶

    Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

    • Demonstrate proficiency in fundamental concepts of Cloud Computing, DevOps, and also its use for AI.
    • Be able to conduct independent practical applications in elementary Cloud Computing, DevOps, and AI-related topics
    • Apply technical tools and frameworks to address real-world problems as demonstrated by open-source student projects.
    • Communicate technical and theoretical findings effectively through structured deliverables, such as documentation, presentations, and a project report.

    Software / Hardware Requirements:¶

    • You can use a variety of resources for this class. Some of them depend on the project the student chooses. This includes:
    • Chameleon Cloud (organized via class):
      • https://chameleoncloud.org/
      • cloudmesh client: CH-817419
    • Access-CI:
      • https://allocations.access-ci.org/
      • CIS261520: Lecture: Cloud Computing, DevOps, and AI
    • Free tiers of public clouds such as AWS, Azure, Google, ...
    • Personal computer on which you have administrative rights and can run
      virtualization software or containers with enough disk space to install IDEs, GitHub, and script environments.

    • Optional Edge Computing device such as Raspberry Pi or Cell phone

    • Important is that you will need a resource that runs containers and virtual machines (we will give further information in class, but most computers these days can do it, or you can use a cloud resource.)

    Please note that there will be free tiers of cloud resources. However you are in charge of budgeting which is part of the educational experience. A cloud can also be simulated on your computer if it is powerfull enough (modern computers will be, they are designed for that).

    Changes to Syllabus

    There may be changes to the syllabus during the semester. You are responsible for all syllabus changes made in class whether or not you attend. Attendance in classes is mandatory.

    Recordings¶

    The classes will be recorded, including your questions. The material will be posted publicly online for everyone to see. This may include questions you ask. If you do not want to be recorded, please remind the instructor to pause the recording. Some lectures may be prerecorded.

    Open Source Project¶

    The class participants benefit from the ability to conduct the project in an open source code repository. Over my many years of teaching, all but one student shared their project online with everyone. Due to this the student could not benefit from collaborative feedback. I encourage online sharing as it shows others what you have done and even allows them to improve your project with proper attributions (obviously this should only be a small portion).

    Group Work for the Project¶

    This class allows group work, and the team size may be based on total class size. At this time, I anticipate the maximum team size is 2 students. However, you can do the class just by yourself. If you work in a group, tasks must be divided equally, and a work breakdown detailing contributions must be submitted with each project report (including the midterm). The group work should be larger than an individual project according to the group size.

    Class Attendance & Course Coverage¶

    You will have the chance to introduce yourself to multiple classmates early in the course. Our actual pace may vary from this schedule: if you miss a class for any reason, it is your responsibility to work through the content, and I also suggest you contact a classmate for further discussion of the topics, as you are still responsible for all material covered and assigned. A few lectures & discussions might sometimes be supplemented by prerecorded content. Slides/handouts/links/animations and other additional resources will be shared. To help you stay on track, lecture notes will be posted and continually updated. Homework objectives are assigned and continually updated. Problems for additional daily practice will be continually updated online.

    We have a Universal Absence Accommodation Policy. Please refer to this with the Grading System, and grade deductions will be done proportionally based on the missed class.

    Assignments¶

    In case a course assignment is missed due to any absence, it must be completed at a later time. All assignments must be completed as part of this class.

    Information about Accessibility Support¶

    Requests for Accommodation: Loyola University Chicago provides reasonable accommodations for students with disabilities. Any student requesting accommodations related to a disability or other condition is required to work together with the Accessibility Center (SAC). Professors will receive an accommodation notification from SAC, preferably within the first two weeks of class. Students are encouraged to meet with their professor individually to discuss their accommodations. All information will remain confidential.

    For more information about registering with SAC or questions about accommodations, please contact SAC at 773-508-3700 or SAC@luc.edu.

    Accommodations for Religious Observances¶

    If you have observances of religious holidays that will cause you to miss class or otherwise affect your academic work in the course, you must alert the instructor no later than Thursday of Week 2 in the semester to request accommodations. Advance notice must be sent to the instructor through Loyola email by this deadline. Loyola University Absence Policy for Students in Co-Curricular Activities (including ROTC). Students missing classes while representing Loyola University Chicago in an official capacity (e.g., intercollegiate athletics, debate team, model government organization) shall be allowed by the faculty member of record to make up any assignments and to receive notes or other written information distributed in the missed classes. Students should discuss with faculty the potential consequences of missing lectures and the ways in which they can be remedied. Students must provide their instructors with proper documentation, i.e., "Athletic Competition & Travel Letter" describing the reason for and date of the absence. This documentation must be signed by an appropriate faculty or staff member, and it must be provided to the professor in the first week of a semester. It is the responsibility of the student to make up any assignments. If the student misses an examination, the instructor is required to allow the student to take the examination at another time (https://www.luc.edu/athleteadvising/attendance.shtml). Students who will miss class for an academic competition or conference must provide proper documentation to their instructor as early in the semester as possible. Advance notice must be sent to the instructor through Loyola email.
